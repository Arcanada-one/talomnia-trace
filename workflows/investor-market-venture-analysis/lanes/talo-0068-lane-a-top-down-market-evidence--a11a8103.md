## Definition drift

**1. The single largest drift in this dataset is "AI agents": embedded-capability vs standalone-product. The source states it outright.**
> "The standalone agentic AI market lands between $7 billion and $8.5 billion. Gartner's broader view, counting agentic capabilities embedded across enterprise software, reaches $201.9 billion in 2026. **That 25x gap is not a contradiction. It is a measurement problem.**"
> — https://softwarestrategiesblog.com/2026/02/26/roundup-of-agentic-ai-forecasts-and-market-estimates-2026/ (HTTP 200)

Market 1 therefore has **two mutually exclusive definitions in circulation**, and they must never be summed or averaged: Gartner USD 201.9–206.5B (2026) counts agentic capability inside enterprise software licences; MarketsandMarkets/Precedence/Fortune Business Insights USD 7–8.5B (2025) count standalone agent products. The 2025 gap between Gartner (USD 86.4B) and the vendor consensus (USD 7.06–7.92B) is ~11×.

**2. Markets 1, 3 and 11 are nested, not adjacent.** Gartner's own May-2026 Table 1 places "AI Software" at USD 453,209M for 2026 inside a USD 2,595,667M total. Gartner's "agent builder platforms" (market 3, USD 5.0B in 2026) is explicitly a **sub-segment of the DSML segment** (USD 29,928M in 2026), which is itself inside the total. Menlo's "Agent Platforms" USD 750M and "AI infrastructure … data storage, retrieval, orchestration" USD 1.5B are both line items inside its USD 37B enterprise genAI total. Summing markets 1, 3 and 11 triple-counts.

**3. Menlo and Gartner are not measuring the same thing at all.** Menlo excludes exactly what Gartner's largest segment is: *"excludes chips (e.g., Nvidia), inference and model serving (e.g., AWS, GCP, Azure, Fireworks), and AI features built into existing software solutions."* Gartner's AI Infrastructure segment is USD 1,431,509M for 2026 — roughly 39× Menlo's entire USD 37B market. Menlo also excludes "AI features built into existing software", which is precisely the USD 201.9B Gartner counts.

**4. Money and adoption point in opposite directions in the same year — report both.** Against Gartner's USD 201.9B of 2026 agentic spend sit: Stanford HAI, *"AI agents specifically remain in early days, with single-digit adoption across most business functions"*; McKinsey, *"in no individual business function are more than 10% scaling them"* and *"only 23% of organizations have scaled agent deployments"*; Menlo, *"Only 16% of enterprise … deployments qualify as true agents"*; Gartner itself, *"40% of projects face cancellation by 2027."*

**5. Markets 2 and 7 are already merging inside supplier P&Ls.** Genpact — a BPO firm — reports: *"Over time, we are also incorporating more digital labor into our workforce in the form of AI agents"* (Form 10-K, HTTP 200). UiPath names outsourcers as the incumbent it displaces: *"we compete with companies that provide and support the traditional systems relying on manual tasks and processes that our platform and products are designed to replace, **including companies that facilitate outsourcing of such tasks and processes to lower cost workers**"* (Form 10-K, HTTP 200). NASSCOM records the same shift on the pricing axis: *"Providers re-engineered revenue models, moving away from FTE delivery to outcome-based, risk-sharing constructs as AI-driven productivity materialises."* AI-workforce revenue and BPO revenue are consequently the **same dollars counted twice** in a naive sum.

**6. Markets 5, 6 and 7 are nested inside market 4 in official statistics, and the nesting is exact.** NAICS 54161 (Management Consulting) ⊂ NAICS 5416 ⊂ NAICS 54 (Professional Services); NAICS 5415 (Computer Systems Design) ⊂ NAICS 54. Census SAS 2022: NAICS 54 = USD 2,841,227M; NAICS 5415 = USD 648,805M; NAICS 54161 = USD 307,310M. BPO/business support (NAICS 5614) sits in **NAICS 56, a different sector entirely**. Aggregators nest them the same way — Precedence segments its *professional services* market as *"Management Consulting Services, Strategy Consulting Services, Cloud Professional Services, Engineering Services"*, and Consultancy.eu states *"the global consulting sector is one of the largest and most mature markets within the professional services industry."*

**7. Market 6 absorbs market 7 in one major source and not in others.** Statista: IT Services = *"IT Consulting & Implementation, Business Process Outsourcing, IT Outsourcing, and Other IT Services"* — so its USD 1.57tn (2026) **already contains** its USD 434.99bn (2026) of BPO, ~28% double-count if summed. NASSCOM and ISG treat them as **siblings**: *"Service Lines: ITO up 2%; Engineering up 35%; BPO down 14%."* Gartner never breaks out BPO at all.

**8. Gartner's IT-services definition changed mid-year.** April 2026: *"IT services, including application implementation and managed services, infrastructure implementation and managed services and IaaS"* → USD 1,870,197M. July 2026: the row is retired and split into *"Services | 1,492 | 3.8 | 1,570 | 5.3"* and *"Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) | 222 | 25.3 | 287 | 29.3"*. Any model anchored on "Gartner IT services = USD 1.87T" is anchored on a definition Gartner abandoned three months later.

**9. Markets 8, 9, 10 leak into each other and into markets 4/5 — the primaries say so.** GLG's S-1: *"Our growth is dependent upon the overall growth of the Insight Network market, which is our core market, as well as the adjacent Research and IDA markets"* and *"GLG is therefore well positioned, and is investing, to accelerate its share of the $42.5 billion market research industry."* Inex One from the other side: *"Expert networks are no longer a luxury for high-stakes deals in high finance; their services are increasingly considered table stakes when running any serious B2B market research"*, and *"Consulting remains the spending engine (~50% industry spend)"* — i.e. roughly half of market 9 is an input cost already booked inside market 5.

**10. Three incompatible units inside market 8 alone.** (a) Client spend/GSV — Upwork USD 4.0B, Fiverr USD 1,073.0M; (b) platform revenue — Upwork USD 787.784M, Fiverr USD 430.9M; (c) addressable work spend — Upwork USD 1.3T. TBRC's USD 8.35B "freelance platforms market" is measured on unit (b). Upwork's USD 1.3T is unit (c) and sits **~156× above the entire aggregator market**, is a **2028 forecast not a 2025 level**, and is by its own footnote *"a commissioned third-party study"*.

**11. Stock vs flow (market 7).** Every ISG number is new-award Annual Contract Value, not revenue. ISG's entire combined market — *"Reached nearly $40B for first time ever"* (Q1 2026) — is ~2% of Gartner's quarterly IT-services spend. Never place them on the same axis.

**12. Value added ≠ turnover ≠ exports.** Eurostat's EUR 779.3B for NACE M is **value added**; the World Bank/IMF USD 1.279tn is **cross-border ICT service exports** (and includes telecom/postal/courier); US Census figures are **revenue**. Three different bases, none comparable to the others.

**13. Employer vs nonemployer moves US professional services by USD 230,259M in 2022 alone** (USD 2,841,227M vs USD 2,610,968M, same source, same year). The QSS quarterly series is employer-only; SAS Table 1 is not.

**14. Big Four "global revenue" is not comparable to Accenture's.** Deloitte USD 70.5B, PwC USD 56.9B, EY USD 53.2B, KPMG USD 39.8B are **unaudited aggregations of legally separate member firms across four different fiscal year-ends** (31 May, 30 Jun, 30 Jun, 30 Sep). Accenture's USD 69,672,977 thousand is SEC-filed audited consolidated revenue.
